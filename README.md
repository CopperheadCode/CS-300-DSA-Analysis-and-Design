5. How has your work on this project evolved the way you write programs that are maintainable, readable, and adaptable?

Writing thorough pseudocode is something I have greatly improved at in this class. I have come to realize that planning a program fully in pseudocode gives you multiple chances to catch cases where the program might fail. It separates coding into multiple phases. In the first phase you research and plan out how something needs to be done. You think of possible failure points in your program and how you can address them, catching edge cases that the compiler may not. You research and learn about your options such as what data structures to use to solve a given problem. Then you move on the second phase, writing code. This phase is benefitted by phase one because the pseudocode is like a step by step instruction manual of what you need to do. Some developers even copy their pseudocode directly into comments in their IDE. While you write the code, you can also re-reason about some concepts in your pseudocode that are questionable possibly leading to a more efficient solution. At compile time, you then get a free round of case and error checking allowing you to fix issues that you didn't see in your pseudocode phase. Overall, by utilizing this two step process, my programs will be more maintainable, readable, and adaptable.
